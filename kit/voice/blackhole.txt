It's Tuesday, and Dave is in space.
He has one job: confirm the wedding cake with Priya. Lemon, or Victoria sponge. He's typing.
Then he falls into a black hole. There are two kinds. We'll do both.
Option one: a stellar black hole. Ten Suns, squeezed into thirty kilometres.
Gravity pulls harder on his feet than his head. Three and a half thousand kilometres out, the difference is ten g. Dave is now taller.
At the horizon it's twenty million g. Dave is a noodle. Physicists call this spaghettification. They are not sorry.
Option two: Sagittarius A-star. Four million Suns, at the centre of our galaxy. The horizon sits twelve million kilometres out. Eighteen Suns in a row.
Out here the tide is a ten-thousandth of a g. Dave crosses the horizon in one piece. He doesn't notice. Still typing.
Inside, every direction leads to the centre. He has about a minute. The phone says: sending.
From outside, a telescope sees something else. Dave slows down. Turns red. Fades. He never quite reaches the edge.
Nothing leaves a black hole. Not light. Not a text. The message is sending. It will be sending forever.
Priya orders the lemon.
Dave would have said lemon. Dave is now very long, or very gone. Either way, the cake is sorted.
