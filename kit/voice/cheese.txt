It's Friday night, and Dave is photographing the Moon.
Priya wants it on the wedding invitation. Dave has one job.
Then the Moon turns into cheese. All of it. Instantly.
Cheese is a third the density of Moon rock. The Moon now weighs a third as much. Same size. Less gravitas.
There is no air. Within a minute, the surface boils dry. The Moon is freeze-dried.
The sunlit side hits one hundred and twenty degrees. The fat melts. Two weeks later that side is minus one thirty, and sets.
At the centre, the pressure is five thousand atmospheres. Food scientists do that on purpose. The core is now a premium product.
Surface gravity drops to a third. Astronauts could jump seven metres. Landing is the problem.
Tides come from the Moon's mass. Every tide on Earth drops by almost half. Boats on mud. Surfers walking home.
Cheese is pale. Moon rock is the colour of a car park. Full moons get five times brighter. Dave's photo is a white circle.
That is enough cheese to feed everyone on Earth for sixteen billion years. The universe is fourteen.
Give it a million years. Micrometeorites and solar wind toast the surface. Dry, dark, cracked.
The Moon is now, technically, a biscuit. Dave gets his photo. Priya says it looks like a biscuit.
