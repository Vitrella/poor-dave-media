It's Saturday at the shopping centre.
Dave is trying on a jumper for the rehearsal dinner. It is one size too small. He is halfway out of it.
Then friction doubles, everywhere on Earth. Every surface grips twice as hard.
The jumper grips twice as hard too. Dave is now committed.
A car at fifty kilometres an hour normally needs fourteen metres to brake. Now it needs seven. So does everyone behind it. Every passenger meets their seatbelt.
A third of a car's fuel already goes on fighting friction. For five seconds, it's two thirds. A billion engines groan at once.
A football that should roll forty metres stops after twenty. Somewhere, a goalkeeper takes the credit.
Rub your hands together and they warm up twice as fast. Dave cannot rub his hands. Dave's hands are inside a jumper.
Every zip being pulled right now stops halfway. Every knot is twice as tight. Every screw is twice as stuck.
Then friction goes back to normal.
Everything that was stuck lets go at once. Including the jumper.
Dave buys the jumper anyway. They have been through a lot together.
