It's Saturday at the seaside.
Dave is finishing a sandcastle. It's a scale model of the wedding venue. Priya has the seating plan.
Then every piece of ice on Earth melts. All of it. In five seconds.
Thirty million cubic kilometres of ice. Antarctica is two kilometres thick. Now it's two kilometres of water, sitting on a continent.
Melting it takes fifteen thousand years of the world's energy, in five seconds. The beach hasn't noticed. The water is at the poles. It's on its way.
It slides off Antarctica as the biggest flood in history, crossing the ocean like a tsunami. Seven hundred kilometres an hour.
Within a day, the sea arrives. It rises seventy metres. A twenty-storey building. Everywhere.
London goes under. New York. Shanghai. Tokyo. Eight of the ten biggest cities on Earth were on the coast.
Floating sea ice adds nothing. It was already in the water. Archimedes did that one in a bath.
Dave has noticed the horizon is higher than the pier. Dave adds a moat.
All that fresh water makes the sea two percent less salty. The fish are fine. Only the fish are fine.
From space, the white bits are gone. Earth gets darker, keeps more sunlight. Not this week's problem.
The sandcastle is no longer a factor. Neither is the venue. Priya keeps the seating plan anyway.
