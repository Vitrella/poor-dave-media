It's Friday, and Dave is late for the rehearsal dinner.
Seven o'clock, the Italian place. Priya's parents came from Leicester. Dave is three kilometres away, with the cake.
So Dave travels at the speed of light. Nearly. Nothing with mass can reach it. That takes infinite energy. He settles for ninety-nine point nine nine nine nine percent.
Getting seventy-five kilos of Dave that fast takes five sextillion joules. Eight years of the planet's energy. Dave had a sandwich.
He passes the restaurant in ten microseconds. Light speed has no brakes.
The Moon in one point three seconds. Jupiter, forty minutes. Pluto, five hours. Dave is still apologising.
At this speed, Dave's time runs seven hundred and seven times slower. One day for Dave is two years for everyone else.
Space squashes flat ahead of him. Five hours to Pluto, on Dave's watch: twenty-five seconds.
Starlight ahead shifts into X-rays. The whole sky bunches into one dot ahead. Behind him: nothing. Dave assumes it's night.
Space has one hydrogen atom per cubic centimetre. Each one hits Dave like a collider beam. Lethal in under a second. He feels a tingle.
At Pluto, Dave turns round. By his watch, he's been gone fifty seconds.
He walks in on time. By his watch. On Earth it's five in the morning. Priya waited. The rehearsal dinner is now a rehearsal breakfast. Dave brought cake.
