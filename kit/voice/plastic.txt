It's Thursday at the tailor's.
Dave is trying on the wedding suit. It's polyester. The tailor calls it close enough.
Then every piece of plastic on Earth vanishes. For five seconds.
The suit goes first. So does the elastic in everyone's underwear. Eight billion people, at once.
Two thirds of the world's clothing fibre is plastic. The other third is suddenly very popular.
Half of every car, by volume, is plastic. Dashboards, bumpers, the insulation on every wire. The wires touch. One and a half billion vehicles short out.
Every cable on Earth does the same. From space, the lights go out.
Ten thousand aircraft are in the air. The newest are half plastic by weight. Now they're half aircraft.
A hundred and forty million contact lenses vanish. So do most spectacle lenses. The world goes blurry.
Every bottle in every supermarket is gone. The drinks hold their shape for a moment. Then they remember.
At sea, a hundred and seventy trillion bits of plastic vanish. The ocean is clean for five seconds. The fish are suspicious.
Dave is in a cotton vest, behind a curtain that was polyester. Priya is studying the ceiling.
Then the plastic comes back. Exactly where it was. Not where things are now.
The suit returns to where Dave was standing. Dave has moved. It stands on its own for a moment. Then it doesn't. The tailor calls it close enough.
