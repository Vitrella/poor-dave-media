Dave has a wedding secret. It needs whispering.
So, the library. Priya is at the far end of the long table. Eight metres. Dave considers that whispering distance.
Then the speed of sound drops to walking pace. One point four metres per second. Two hundred and forty-five times slower.
Dave whispers. The whisper leaves his mouth at walking pace. Dave walks alongside it.
Anything faster than a stroll is now supersonic. Your foot swings at Mach three. Every step is a sonic boom. About a thunderclap.
The librarian shushes him. Her breath leaves at Mach twenty. The shush is a sonic boom.
Outside, a car at fifty is Mach ten, dragging a shock wave down the street. A hundred and forty decibels, per car.
Rain falls at Mach six. Pigeons cruise at Mach fourteen. Bats outrun their own echoes. The bats are concerned.
Forty-four lightning bolts a second, worldwide. For five seconds, none of them have thunder. The thunder is walking.
Then sound comes back. Everything said in those five seconds sits within seven metres of whoever said it. It arrives in twenty milliseconds, pitched up two hundred and forty-five times.
Nine hundred million dogs hear five seconds of humanity in one click. They are not impressed.
Dave's whisper reaches Priya as a click. The secret is safe. The dog knows.
